KC Ohaebosim Becomes First Nigerian-American Elected US Lieutenant Governor

By Trending9ja Staff • November 13, 2026 • 1 min read

KC Ohaebosim, a Wichita Democrat, has made history by becoming the first Nigerian-American elected as a lieutenant governor in the United States, following his victory in the Kansas state elections, Punch reported.

The head of the Nigerians in Diaspora Commission, Abike Dabiri-Erewa, shared the news on X to congratulate Ohaebosim. She celebrated his milestone achievement after officials confirmed his victory following an extended verification process.

The result was announced more than a week after the November 3 voting due to a tight audit. Kansas City public radio station KCUR reported that Ohaebosim ran on a ticket with Governor-elect Cindy Holscher. The Democratic duo narrowly beat Republican candidate Ty Masterson, the Kansas Senate President, by around 0.6 percent or under 8,000 votes.

Ohaebosim was born in Wichita, Kansas, to Nigerian physician Dr. Linus Ohaebosim. He spent part of his early life and primary schooling in Nigeria before returning to Kansas. He attended Wichita Heights High School, earned an undergraduate degree at Wichita State University, and completed a master's degree in business administration at Newman University.

Before entering public service, Ohaebosim worked as a data analyst in technology. Since 2017, he served in the Kansas House of Representatives for District 89, participating in committees on transportation, budget, and energy. He decided not to seek another term in the state House to join Holscher's campaign.

His win marks another historic milestone, as he becomes the second Black person elected to statewide office in Kansas after Edward McCabe in 1882. Punch reported that Ohaebosim and Holscher will assume their executive duties in 2027.
