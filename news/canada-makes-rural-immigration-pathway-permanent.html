Canada Makes Rural Immigration Pathway Permanent For Foreign Workers

By Trending9ja Staff • November 10, 2026 • 1 min read

Canada's government has converted its rural immigration pilot into a permanent pathway to address ongoing workforce shortages in smaller towns. According to Vanguard, the Minister of Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship, Lena Metlege Diab, revealed the policy change on Monday, November 9, 2026, during an event in Pictou County, Nova Scotia.

A statement from Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada indicated that the initiative will initially maintain the 14 participating localities from the trial phase, with provisions for future expansion. The employer-driven project links skilled international professionals and their households with regional recruiters and integration support services, encouraging long-term residence outside major cities.

Under the country's 2027–2029 Immigration Levels Plan, authorities have reserved 5,500 residency slots for the permanent rural track. Vanguard noted that the measure forms part of broader policy goals to balance overall immigration intake, lower temporary resident totals, and retain foreign talent currently assisting local businesses.

Early evaluations between 2025 and 2026 revealed that immigrant workers filled critical staffing gaps in health care, teaching, sales, and service industries. Furthermore, local authorities vetted and approved more than 1,950 verified employers between January 2025 and August 2026 after those businesses failed to fill openings using the domestic labour pool.

Sources: Vanguard
