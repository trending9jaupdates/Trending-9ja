World Bank Highlights $4tn Funding Gap Facing Small Businesses

By Trending9ja Staff • November 17, 2026 • 1 min read

Micro, small, and medium enterprises across developing nations are grappling with a massive $4 trillion funding deficit, according to a recent World Bank report cited by Punch. This deficit, recorded between 2019 and 2023, severely restricts smaller firms from scaling their operations and creating vital job opportunities.

The international financial institution pointed out that small businesses make up roughly 90 percent of enterprises and employ 75 percent of the labour force in emerging markets. Consequently, unlocking credit for them is vital as approximately 1.2 billion young citizens are projected to enter the workforce over the coming decade to fifteen years.

According to findings by the global lender, gaining access to structured loans delivers measurable benefits to small operations. Enterprises that manage to secure formal credit typically experience a 12 percent rise in workforce numbers, while their overall sales and profits expand by roughly 18 percent.

World Bank Group Vice President for Prosperity, Pablo Saavedra, explained that these firms struggle with four major bottlenecks. He noted that many operators remain invisible to traditional banks due to a lack of digital financial records, suffer isolation from major markets, struggle with complex regulations, and continuously lack sufficient funding.

To help bridge this shortfall, the World Bank introduced an intervention plan designed to mobilise commercial lenders and private investors. The strategy combines digital transaction tracking, equity investments, and risk-sharing models while urging national authorities to cut red tape, especially in fragile and conflict-affected territories.

The findings, contained in the organisation's Finance and Prosperity 2026 report, also highlighted the broader vulnerabilities facing emerging financial systems. The report urged financial regulators to strengthen banking supervision and safety nets amid global economic uncertainties and sovereign debt pressures.

Sources: Punch
