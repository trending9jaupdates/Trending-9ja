MTN and Telkom Reopen South Africa Acquisition Talks After Four Years

By Trending9ja Staff • November 25, 2026 • 1 min read

MTN Group and South African telecommunications provider Telkom have reopened acquisition discussions four years after earlier negotiations collapsed, according to TechCabal. A successful transaction would combine two major mobile operations and create a dominant force in South Africa's fibre and telecommunications market.

The proposed deal comes as telecom operators face high capital demands for spectrum, network coverage, base stations, and power infrastructure. TechCabal reported that combining both operators could help eliminate duplicated network spending and direct more resources toward expanding 5G networks and fixed-line enterprise services.

Addressing the need for market consolidation, MTN Group Chief Executive Officer Ralph Mupita previously told consulting firm McKinsey that operating four or more full-infrastructure networks in one market makes sustainable financial returns difficult. Mupita predicted that telecom markets would gradually shift toward having two or three major operators.

However, TechCabal noted that absorbing Telkom could remove an independent infrastructure competitor, potentially concentrating digital infrastructure control. Without regulatory oversight, this shift could limit options for smaller internet service providers and affect pricing for everyday consumers.

According to Telkom's latest interim results highlighted by TechCabal, the company generated R22.5 billion ($1.3 billion) in group revenue, a 2 percent increase, while earnings before interest, tax, depreciation, and amortisation grew 5.8 percent to R6.4 billion ($377 million). Telkom's mobile business contributed R13.3 billion ($764 million), with mobile data users rising to 21 million, and its fibre division, Openserve, expanded connected homes by 15.1 percent to 870,856.

Sources: TechCabal
